Debt Management Strategies

Debt Avalanche Method:
Pay minimum amounts on all debts, then put all extra money toward the debt with the highest interest rate.
Most mathematically efficient — minimises total interest paid over time.
Best for: people motivated by numbers and long-term savings.
Example: If you have a credit card at 36% and a personal loan at 14%, attack the credit card first.

Debt Snowball Method:
Pay minimum amounts on all debts, then put all extra money toward the debt with the smallest balance.
Provides psychological wins early by eliminating accounts faster.
Best for: people who need motivation and visible progress.
May cost more in total interest than avalanche method.

Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI):
Monthly debt payments divided by gross monthly income.
Below 20%: healthy — good financial flexibility
20-35%: manageable — monitor carefully
35-50%: concerning — prioritise debt reduction
Above 50%: critical — seek financial counselling

Credit Card Debt:
Average credit card interest rate in India: 30-42% annually
Always pay more than the minimum — minimum payments barely cover interest
Minimum payment trap: paying only minimums on Rs 1,00,000 at 36% takes over 10 years to clear
Balance transfer cards: can reduce interest temporarily but watch for transfer fees

Personal Loan vs Credit Card Debt:
Personal loans typically charge 10-24% interest — significantly lower than credit cards
Consider personal loan to consolidate high-interest credit card debt
Ensure the personal loan EMI fits comfortably within budget

Good Debt vs Bad Debt:
Good debt: home loan (asset appreciation + tax benefits), education loan (income potential)
Bad debt: credit card revolving balance, high-interest personal loans for consumption
Neutral debt: car loan (depreciating asset but necessary for many)

Debt Freedom Order (recommended):
1. High-interest credit card balances (36%+)
2. Personal loans (14-24%)
3. Car loans (8-12%)
4. Home loan (7-9%) — lowest priority, tax benefits apply
