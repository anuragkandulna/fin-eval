Savings and Investment Basics

Emergency Fund First:
Before any investment, build 3-6 months of expenses in a liquid account.
No investment return justifies skipping the emergency fund — a single emergency can derail years of investing.

Savings Rate Benchmarks:
- Minimum: 10% of take-home income
- Recommended: 20% of take-home income
- Aggressive wealth building: 30-40% of take-home income
- FIRE (Financial Independence): 50%+ savings rate

Compound Interest:
Interest earned on both principal and accumulated interest.
Rule of 72: divide 72 by annual return rate to estimate doubling time.
Example: at 8% annual return, money doubles approximately every 9 years (72/8=9).
Starting early matters more than the amount — time is the most powerful variable.

Investment Options in India (General Information Only):
PPF (Public Provident Fund): Government-backed, 15-year lock-in, tax-free returns, Section 80C benefit
EPF (Employees Provident Fund): Mandatory for salaried employees, employer matches contribution
NPS (National Pension System): Long-term retirement savings, partial tax-free withdrawal at retirement
Mutual Funds: Pooled investment vehicle — returns vary based on fund type and market conditions
Fixed Deposits: Capital-protected, lower returns, suitable for conservative investors

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan):
Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals (typically monthly) in mutual funds.
Reduces impact of market timing through rupee cost averaging.
Even small amounts (Rs 500/month) benefit from starting early.

Important Disclaimer:
Past performance of any investment does not guarantee future returns.
All investment decisions should be based on individual risk tolerance, time horizon, and financial goals.
Consult a SEBI-registered financial advisor for personalised investment advice.
This information is educational only and not financial advice.

Inflation Consideration:
Indian inflation has historically averaged 5-6% annually.
Any savings earning less than inflation rate is losing real value over time.
Keeping large amounts in low-interest savings accounts erodes purchasing power.
