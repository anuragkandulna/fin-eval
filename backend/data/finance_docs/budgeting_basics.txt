Personal Budgeting Fundamentals

The 50/30/20 Rule:
A simple budgeting framework allocating after-tax income as follows:
- 50% to Needs: rent/EMI, groceries, utilities, minimum debt payments, insurance
- 30% to Wants: dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, travel, hobbies
- 20% to Savings and Debt Repayment: emergency fund, investments, extra debt payments

Emergency Fund Guidelines:
- Recommended size: 3-6 months of essential living expenses
- For self-employed or variable income: 6-12 months recommended
- Keep in liquid, accessible accounts — savings account or liquid mutual fund
- Build emergency fund before investing in equity

Zero-Based Budgeting:
Every rupee of income is assigned a purpose until income minus allocations equals zero.
Unlike the 50/30/20 rule, zero-based budgeting requires itemising every expense category.
Better for people with irregular income or those trying to find hidden spending.

Pay Yourself First:
Automatically transfer savings amount on payday before spending on anything else.
Treats savings as a non-negotiable expense rather than whatever is left over.
Recommended for people who struggle to save consistently.

Budget Review Frequency:
- Monthly review: track actual vs planned spending
- Quarterly review: adjust categories based on life changes
- Annual review: revise savings goals and major expense categories

Common Budget Mistakes:
- Forgetting irregular expenses (annual insurance premiums, car service)
- Not accounting for lifestyle inflation as income grows
- Setting unrealistic targets that lead to abandoning the budget
- Not tracking small daily expenses that add up significantly
